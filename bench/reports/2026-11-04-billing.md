## What the results show

- **The temptations are the clearest signal.** Without memory, the three models always do what they are asked, even when it contradicts a decision or the charter (30 of 30). With kontext, 6 to 8 of 30. Claude Code's automatic memory helps with scope (9 of 15 on WhatsApp), but not with the Postmark decision (15 of 15): it doesn't store it as something that binds.
- **kontext comes out a little cheaper**, not by much: with opus, 0.51 USD per run (`kontext-inject`) and 0.60 (`kontext-pointer`) against 0.66 without kontext; with sonnet, 0.26–0.28 against 0.29; with haiku, 0.02 against 0.03. `notes` ends up close to kontext.
- **Drift % is very noisy.** It depends on whether the agent undoes the change after the correction, and with 5 seeds the standard deviation is as large as the mean. With opus everything is at 0 except `none`.
- **Memory % doesn't tell the variants apart.** Almost everything is between 93 and 100 %: day 1 puts the charter in the conversation, and the questions are easy. The kontext variants do reach 100 % on every model.
- **Finished** is 100 % everywhere: the scenario is too small to tell apart the quality of the work.

## Limits, before publishing

- A 3-day scenario is not "weeks". Longer scenarios are missing, with decisions that change (supersede) and several agents.
- The temptations are explicit requests from the user. The harness rewards the agent for remembering the decision instead of obeying. That is what kontext aims for, but it can be argued.
- The memory questions have a ceiling: we need questions about earlier days and about things that changed.
- In some transcripts the agent could not run compound commands (`cd … && ctx propose`, `PYTHONPATH=src python3 …`) because of permissions. This affects all variants equally.
- n = 5 per cell; there are no significance tests.
